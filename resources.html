For Programs

Data That Tells Your Story

Early childhood programs do invisible work. The relationships built, the crises navigated, the moments of growth that never show up in a spreadsheet—these are the heart of what you do. But funders, policy makers, and communities often can’t see what they can’t measure.

Good data doesn’t just count things. It asks the right questions: What changed? For whom? Because of what? Our data specialist Anja Mayr offers a few starting points for programs that want to tell a truer story.

Start with your theory of change. Before collecting anything, ask: what do we believe our work causes to happen? Be honest about the chain of logic. When you can name it, you can measure it.

Collect less, use more. Most programs collect more data than they ever act on. Choose three to five indicators that actually reflect the change you’re trying to create—and collect them consistently.

Let families and educators tell it too. Quantitative data answers “how much”. Qualitative data answers “so what.” A short story from a parent, a quote from an educator, a before-and-after observation—these make your numbers breathe.
